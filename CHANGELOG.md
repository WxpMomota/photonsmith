# Changelog

Release notes for the next tagged release, summarising the large correctness effort of October 2026.
Before this, see `git log`. Each item has a commit with the measurements behind it, and
[docs/PBRT_SUPPORT.md](docs/PBRT_SUPPORT.md) has the per-feature, per-backend detail.

## Unreleased (after 0.1.5)

* **Metal draws a window's picture** (an `AreaLightSource` with a `"filename"` on any shape, which is how Scene Builder's Light picture and Room from a photo write a window: a thin emissive box): Metal kept ONE picture per scene and read it on a single quad, so a box window was one flat colour and a second window's picture was lost. All the pictures of a scene now sit in one atlas, each light reads its own, a mesh light reads it at the mesh's own uv, and light sampling does too. A new CPU-versus-Metal group (`lightpic`, in ctest: three pictures on two box windows and a quad window, and a picture on a box) reads 0.03 against the CPU, 0.32 before. A picture on a box's surface (a room's floor and walls) already agreed.

* **Scene Builder: Match to a photo... for a Sun & sky light** (under Sky gallery; plain image analysis, nothing to install, `src/shared/light_from_photo.h`): a photo of an outdoor scene sets the sun's height and direction and the haze. The sky is found as the blue or white region hanging from the top of the picture, the camera's tilt is read off the horizon (or given), the sun is read straight off the picture when it is in it (a small clipped spot), and otherwise the Hosek-Wilkie sky the Builder renders with is fitted to the sky's brightness in each direction, up to one gain since the exposure is not known. The direction is set relative to where the Builder camera looks. Checked on 64 skies made from the model with known suns (the mean error is under 5 degrees in height and direction, the sun in the picture or not, with a building hiding part of the sky), by a headless GUI test, and on nine real photos from the web (no ground truth, judged by eye: it gave the sun where it looks in the two photos with a visible sun, plausible front-lit suns on a beach and a canyon rim, and a low confidence, saying so, on stormy, cloudy and thin-sky photos). The real photos found three faults, fixed with tests: a blue sea counted as sky, bright cloud tops taken for the sun, and a near-perfect confidence from a thin strip of sky. A review found that an even blue sky was called overcast (it is not any more: it is blue) and that a portrait photo was read with a landscape photo's field of view (the dialog now starts at 51 degrees for one). A low hazy sun, a sunset sky (the sky finder looks for blue and white) and an overcast sky (no gradient: it says so) are not read; the brightness stays as it was.

* **Scene Builder: Match to a photo... for the camera** (at the top of the camera's properties; plain image analysis, nothing to install, `src/shared/camera_from_photo.h`): the camera takes a photo's lens (read from the photo's EXIF data when it has it: the 35 mm equivalent focal length, with a bounds-checked reader tested against truncated and damaged files; else a phone's), its tilt and roll (read off the horizon, the lower edge of the sky, by a line fit that drops the points a building or tree raises), a height you give, and keeps the heading it has; the picture takes the photo's shape, the 3D view looks through the camera and the photo is laid over it (opacity, show and remove in the Camera section) to line a scene up against it; a tick box sets the Sun & sky from the same photo. Light from a photo's field of view now also starts from the photo's EXIF data. Checked on photos from a camera of known tilt and roll (within a degree), by a headless GUI test, and by rendering a scene through the camera it makes: the horizon lands where the photo's did, so the signs of the tilt and the roll are right through the pbrt file and the renderer. Tried on nine real photos (no ground truth): clean sea and heath horizons gave plausible values; hills, dunes and roof edges warn that the horizon is not a clean line. A horizon hidden by buildings is read as the skyline; there is no shadow catcher or photo backdrop in the final render (the photo is laid over the view in the Builder; composite in an image editor).

* **Scene Builder: Render into the photo...** (in the Camera section, under Match to a photo..., which it needs first; `src/shared/photo_composite.h`, `qt_gui/scene_builder_composite.cpp`, nothing to install, no renderer change): the scene is rendered three times through the camera matched to the photo (as it is, without the inserted objects, and as a mask of them) and put into the photo with the shadows and reflections it casts on the ground: the ratio of the first two, in linear light, multiplies the photo; the objects are un-matted at their edges and pasted in, tone mapped as the renderer shows pictures (measured against its PNG) with an exposure control. The ground the photo already has is ticked in the dialog (a floor named Floor or Ground starts ticked) and is not drawn; lights are never pasted. Render noise is kept out of the photo by a smoothing and a small dead-band, so the far field is exactly the photo's pixels. Tested with synthetic pictures of known answers, by rendering a sphere and a sun through the launcher, and by a headless GUI test (`composite`). **Match the objects' exposure and colour to the photo's sky** (`photo_composite::matchToPhoto`; ticked when the scene has a Sun & sky): the exposure and the colour cast that bring the rendered sky to the photo's sky (read pixel by pixel from the photo's own sky, median, objects and clipped pixels left out) are given to the objects, with a clear message when the photo's sky is not like the scene's (mostly cloud, no Sun & sky set from the photo) instead of a guess; checked with synthetic skies of known exposure and cast, with clouds, trees and objects in the way, and on real photos (a clear beach matched, an overcast heath declined). The camera-photo note about the Sun & sky no longer shows a doubled ampersand.

* **Scene Builder: place objects on the photo's ground by clicking** (`Place the picked object by clicking...` in an object's properties and in the Arrange menu; `scene_doc::placeOnGround`, `heightOfItems`, `setHeightOfItems` in `src/shared/scene_arrange.h`): with the camera matched to a photo, each click on the ground in the camera view stands the picked object (or objects, together) there, the middle of its base under the click and its lowest point on the ground; **Set the picked object's real height...** scales it to a height in metres about the middle of its base. The view's new Place mode (`Scene3DView::beginPickPlace`) takes every click until Esc, as Pick focus takes one. Checked by unit tests and by a headless GUI test that sends a real mouse click.

* **Scene Builder: sample photos for the photo features**: Match to a photo (for the camera and for the Sun & sky) opens a picker with five free-licence photos built into the program (beach, North Sea beach, heath under clouds, partly cloudy meadow, overcast hills; scaled to 1280 pixels across, `qt_gui/sample_photos/`, `resources.qrc`), each with a picture, a note on what it is good for and its credit, or **Choose my own photo...**; after a sample is chosen for the camera, the message says what to do next. Credits in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and `docs/PHOTO_TO_SCENE.md`. Checked by the `composite` self-test: every sample is in the program, sets a camera and a sky, the picker opens and cancels, and the beach is put into with its sky giving the exposure.

* **Scene Builder: what it is doing now shows in the Log Output tab**: the Scene Builder gained a `logMessage` signal that the main window shows in the Log Output pane (and keeps in the log file, as it keeps everything the pane shows). Render into the photo reports its three renders as they run (the command of each, its progress in quarters, the renderer's own lines, the time of each, the exposure match and the picture saved, or why it failed), with the progress window now showing each render's percentage; Match to a photo for the camera and the Sun & sky, and placing an object, report what they read and did; the Builder's preview, final-picture and video renders log their start, the renderer's output when they fail, and their end in the pane too (before, those went only to the log file). The `composite` self-test checks the pane shows the three renders and the saved picture.

* **Scene Builder: Render into the photo shows its picture in the app**: the result appears in the preview pane (below the 3D view, scrolled into view), like a Preview, instead of being saved to a file you have to choose first; the new **Save picture...** button (next to Preview) saves whatever the preview pane shows, a Preview too, as a PNG. A new **Preview in the photo** button renders at the Preview's Quality with no questions (the ground, exposure and matching last used in the dialog), for use while placing objects. Checked by the `composite` self-test (the picture is in the pane at the Draft size).

* **Scene Builder: Render into the photo - say what each object is**: the dialog lists every object with a choice (Put into the photo / Ground / Light) instead of ticks that left lamps out, remembered by name for Preview in the photo; the first guess no longer takes any object whose name merely begins with "floor" or "ground" for the ground (an object made from a photo takes its file's name: "floor_lamp.jpg" made a "floor_lamp" that was guessed to be the floor, and with the lamp a light left nothing to put into the photo); the error now lists what each object was taken to be and where to change it. Log lines the Builder sends to the Log Output pane are labelled once ("Warning:", not "[WARN] ... [WARN]").

* **Scene Builder: Render into the photo and Preview in the photo can denoise**: with the Denoise tick next to Preview on (also a tick in the Render into the photo dialog, the same setting), the "as it is" and "without the objects" renders are denoised (NVIDIA's denoiser on the GPU, Open Image Denoise on the CPU) before the objects are put into the photo, so a low sample count looks clean; the mask is left alone. When the denoiser cannot run the picture is made without it and the message says so. The `composite` self-test measures the grain with and without it (where a denoiser exists: on the GPU, `RT_GUI_SELFTEST_COMPOSITE_GPU=1`, it fell from 2.60 to 0.72) and checks the message where there is none.

* **GUI starts in under a second again (it took 10 to 22 seconds with a full output folder)**: at start-up the Preview tab builds its "Recent Renders" list, and it made an entry (which asks the scene list about the file's scene, some 35 ms each) for every `render_*.png` found in the default folders, <exe_dir>/output (the command line and the tests write there too) and the Pictures and Desktop folders, to keep only the newest twenty. With 595 renders found that was 22 s before the window opened. The files are now listed and sorted by date first and only the newest twenty get an entry (595 files: 6 ms). The log's start-up profile now also says what the restore of the unsaved scene and the recent-renders list cost. The `ui` self-test makes 400 render files and checks that the list is built in under 3 s from them (`RAY_TRACER_RECENT_SCAN_DIR` points the scan at a test folder).

* **Scene Builder: the result of Match to a photo is shown in the same window** (the camera's and the Sun & sky's): pressing Ok no longer closes the settings and opens a second pop-up with what was read; the settings page gives way, in the same window, to the result (scrolling when long) and a single Close button, and a photo that cannot be read leaves the settings to be changed. One shared helper (`qt_gui/photo_dialog.h`). The `composite` self-test drives the whole of it with real clicks: the picker, the settings, Ok, the result, Close.

* **Scene Builder: Match to a photo is one window from start to finish** (the camera's and the Sun & sky's): the photo to use (the five samples as a row of pictures, or one's own, which joins the row), the settings and then the result are all in the same window, where the photo chooser used to be a window of its own ahead of the settings (`sample_photos::Picker`, built into the dialogs; changing the photo updates the field of view that its lens data gives). The `composite` self-test drives the window: the samples listed, one's own photo chosen (twice), Ok, the result, Close.

* **Scene Builder: the result of Match to a photo appears beside the settings**, not instead of them: the photo row and settings stay on the left, the result in a panel on the right of the same window; Ok then reads "Apply again" (change a setting and read it again) and Cancel reads "Close". The settings are in a scroll area and the window is sized to the screen, so a short screen can still reach all of them. The `composite` self-test checks a failure leaves things as they were, the result appears while the settings stay, a second Apply replaces the result, and the buttons are relabelled.

* **Scene Builder: Match to a photo applies on click** (the camera's and the Sun & sky's window): no Ok or "Apply again" button; clicking a photo in the row (or one's own file, or an arrow key) sets the camera or the sky from it at once, and changing a setting applies again after a short pause (typing a number is one apply); the result is in the panel beside the settings, and an error shows there too, not in a pop-up. Each apply takes the window's last one back first, so trying five photos leaves one apply in the undo list. `photo_dialog::run` now takes the signals to wire and runs the apply after the pause; `SceneBuilderWidget::undoDepth()/undoTo()`. The `composite` self-test clicks through the real window (hint first, a click, an arrow key, one's own file) and checks the panel and the undo list; a probe checks nothing is applied before the first click and two quick changes are one apply.

* **Scene Builder: the Match to a photo window opens large**: about two thirds of the screen's width by 92% of its height, centred (it had been sized to its contents, about half a 2000-pixel-wide screen), with the result **under** the settings, not beside them (two fifths of the height); the introduction moved into the result panel (until there is a result) so the settings fit without scrolling; the photo row, its credit and the settings are more compact; the settings wrap instead of scrolling sideways; the window can be resized. The `composite` self-test checks it opens tall (85% of the screen's height).

* **Review fixes for the photo features** (found by reading the changes since Camera from a photo): **Set real height and Place on the ground treated a mesh by the bottom of its bounding sphere**, so an Object from a photo was lifted or sunk when scaled (a mesh now stands on its origin, 2 scale units tall, as the photo-to-object helper makes it; lights no longer decide where a pick stands); **Place mode** swallowed every click until Esc: it now ends when the camera's view is left or the view is hidden, and says on the picture that it is on; **Preview in the photo / Render into the photo** could start while another render was running (the button is off during one and the render refuses); the composite renders in **the photo's shape** whatever the scene's Image size says now, and says so; the **result text and the credit label are plain text** (a file name with markup was shown as rich text); the window is **sized for the screen it is on**; and the apply-and-undo of the photo windows survives a **full undo list** (`SnapshotHistory::serial()` keeps counting when the size limit drops old steps).

* **Scene Builder: Match to a photo has an Apply button** next to Close (Enter applies too): clicking a photo and changing a setting still apply by themselves, and Apply does it now, without the short pause, for anyone who wants to be sure. The self-test checks it applies at once.

## 0.1.5 (2026-10-16)

* **The first Metal render after an install waits about two minutes, not four**: Metal turns the path tracer into machine code once per Mac, for two shader variants (the second is for alpha masks and subsurface), and the app built them one after the other (108 s then 121 s on an M2). They are now built side by side (about 125 s in all: `ray_tracer --prepare-gpu` 231 s -> 136 s, measured with a cold cache). A Live Preview that starts during the warm-up still waits for the build of its own variant instead of compiling it twice.

* **The OptiX recursive backend is six PTX modules, not one** (`optix_programs.cu` is the core, `optix_programs_{sphere,quad,disk,cylinder,triangle}.cu` hold each shape): the single 55 MB module took 171 s in one compiler process, the six compile side by side in about 76 s, so editing a device header costs about 85 s of build instead of 175 s. The first run after a device edit still compiles the modules for the GPU once (about 250 s, as before; `optixModulesCreateParallel` shares that work across every core). Packages now carry all six `.ptx` files.
* **The test runner has a Render tier** (`scripts/run_tests_parallel.ps1 -Tier Render`): the CPU-render suites and the Live Preview tests no longer sit in the "Fast" tier, which went from timing out at 1800 s to 40 s; `-Tier Split` (Fast, then Render and Slow at once) takes about six minutes with a warm OptiX cache.
* **The pbrt reference pack is tighter**: the tolerance floor is 1.5% instead of 3% (`--min-tol`), so a quiet GPU scene is held to 1.5% of its measured offset (the CPU's floor is 2.5%: a 1.5% CPU band failed a full run at +1.5% on a scene that reads within 0.4% alone); expected values did not move. (The CPU's noisier rows are 6 sigma wide, not 4: a heavy-tailed scene failed a full run at +3.5% against 3.2% while reading within 0.6% in three runs on its own.)
* **Scene Builder: Add > Room from a photo...** (optional, the same helper environment as *Object from a photo*; nothing is uploaded): one photo of a room becomes an editable room, a first draft to tidy up. Guide: `docs/PHOTO_TO_SCENE.md`.
  * **The room**: a small depth model (Depth Anything V2, Metric Indoor Small, about 100 MB downloaded on first use) guesses distances; `tools/photo_to_room/room_fit.py` levels the camera by the floor, squares the room up by its walls, reads the floor, ceiling and the walls the photo shows, takes each surface's colour from the photo, turns patches clipped to white (windows, lamps) into emitting panels and puts the camera where the photo was taken from. A room that is not one rectangle (an L, an alcove, a bay, a window set in a deep recess) is read as an outline of straight walls at right angles (`room_outline.py`); sloping ceilings and walls at other angles are not modelled. The Builder then renders a small draft of the room and sets the lights until its brightness matches the photo's (the open-loop guess was several times off); the progress window has a Cancel button that keeps the helper's guess.
  * **The photo's own look** (`room_textures.py`): each point of the floor, ceiling and walls is looked up in the photo with the fitted camera, so a wood floor keeps its grain, tiles their grout and a painting on a wall comes with it; furniture in front of a surface is left out and the hole is filled smoothly. A window shows what the photo shows there: its pixels are cut out as the panel's picture, through the Builder's new **Light picture** (a glowing object can take its colours from a picture; written as pbrt's image area light, honoured by the CPU and both OptiX backends on every shape).
  * **Furniture** (**Furniture to add**, 4 to start with; a second small model, DETR, about 170 MB, names what is in the photo; `room_objects.py`): a table is a measured top (read from the rays through its pixels, carrying the photo's own picture of it) on four legs; a fridge, cabinet, counter or bed is a measured block; stairs are the Builder's stairs object, measured (run, climb direction, rise, about 18 cm steps); the rest (chairs, sofas, plants...) is cut out and the Object-from-a-photo model makes a 3D mesh of it, stood on the floor where the photo shows it, turned flat if the mesh came out leaning, kept to a believable height and facing the camera. Objects in the room are left out of the wall and floor pictures.
  * **Limits**: furniture is a guess (a piece cut off by the picture's edge or half hidden comes out poorly, thin legs are lost, the back is invented); walls the photo does not show are assumed and listed; the size follows the depth model's scale unless you give the camera height; a very wide-angle photo of a small room is read as a longer, narrower room than it is (set the field of view). Not tried on a Mac: `docs/GPU_SCENE_COMMON.md` says what to check (on Metal a window's picture shows as a flat colour).
  * **Tried on**: rooms of known size photographed analytically (turned, tilted, rolled, noisy depth), three rendered interiors, a real photo (a double-height room with a staircase and a loft) and eight photos from the web with free licences (bedrooms, living rooms, a kitchen). What that found and fixed: a doorway taken for the back wall (a wall must now be 1.2 m wide), chairs under 1% of the picture missed, a staircase smeared, a bed read as 18 cm high, sofas that leaned and floated, a table shown as a white square.
  * **Review fixes**: the camera rolled on a pitched corner shot (its up was not turned with the room: 3 to 10 degrees); a camera height that could not be read left the room 10 cm out from under the camera (a typed 0.15 m shrank it tenfold); a failed furniture stage (no network, out of memory) lost the whole room; a ceiling could sit at or below a 6 m camera; a field of view the Builder would refuse was found only after the furniture was made; a nearly black photo was matched to a mid-grey; a room file naming a picture or mesh outside its folder, or an absurd camera or count, was trusted; the light-level step froze the window for up to seven minutes.
* **A Windows user or folder name with an accented or non-Latin letter works** (a profile named `José`, a scene in a folder named in Chinese): the renderer and the GUI keep paths as UTF-8, but Windows read the narrow-string paths of a program in the system's ANSI code page, so such a folder was a different, missing folder: the renderer could not open a scene file given by such a path and silently skipped every mesh and picture inside one (found by rendering a room made from a photo in a folder named `José 用户 café`). Both programs now carry `resources/utf8.manifest` (`activeCodePage` UTF-8; Windows 10 version 1903 or later, earlier versions keep the old behaviour), added to every program project by `Directory.Build.props` and to the GUI by `RayTracerGUI.pro`. Checked on the CPU and the GPU, and by making a room from a photo kept in such a folder.
* **Scene Builder: Make from a photo... for a material** (under **Picture**, matte and glossy paint; plain image processing, nothing to install, `src/shared/material_from_photo.h`): one photo of a surface (wood, tile, fabric, a wall) becomes a colour picture with the photo's uneven light divided out and made to repeat with no joins (the patch is laid over a copy of itself shifted by half, and the join is cut along the path where the two nearly agree, so lines and grain stay sharp, with the brightness difference at the cut spread into the copy; where the two agree nowhere better than anywhere else, a ramp of light, they are blended instead), a bump map guessed from the picture at two scales, the average colour, the gloss and relief a kind of surface usually has (varnished wood, tile and leather get a clear coat) and the repeat count from the width of the photographed patch. The dialog previews the picture repeated twice across and down and the bump map. Checked with hand-made photos (a lit-from-one-side plank pattern, grooves, a ramp: the light is taken out, the join is gone, the grooves are low) and on three crops of the web photos (a wood floor, a stone floor, a wooden ceiling) rendered on a large quad. The relief is a guess from the colours; the gloss is a starting value, not measured. Not tried on a Mac.
* **README and gallery refreshed**: all twelve renders redone with the current build (eleven look the same, the Final scene is brighter and less noisy), a row of three Cornell material scenes and the Studio Showcase added, the social preview carries the Photonsmith name, and the counts (66 test scenes, nearly 6,000 tests) are current.
* Looked at and left (recorded in `docs/PBRT_SUPPORT.md`): the 2-4% excess of chromatic absorbers in the RGB renderers (pbrt averages over wavelengths; the spectral wavefront backend already agrees) and the flat-shaded OptiX strands.

**Known in 0.1.5 on the Mac (Metal):** nothing new was run on a Mac. Room from a photo (its helper's setup script, the draft-render light level) and Light picture are untested there; on Metal a window's picture in a room made from a photo shows as the picture (see the Unreleased entry; it was one flat colour), and Make from a photo for a material is plain Qt and C++ and works unchanged (the GUI self-test `material` passes on the Mac). `docs/GPU_SCENE_COMMON.md` ("Room from a photo: what the Mac has to check") lists the checks.

## 0.1.4 (2026-10-15)

This release is about what the new pbrt reference pack showed: every bundled scene pbrt-v4 can read now has pbrt's own mean in a table the tests read, and the differences that surfaced were fixed. Highlights, with the full list below:

* **The pbrt reference pack** (`tests/data/pbrt_reference_means.txt`, `scripts/pbrt_reference_pack.py`): 143 scenes, and the CPU and both OptiX backends are tested against pbrt's numbers without pbrt installed. Scenes that differed from pbrt by more than 1.5% went from 24 to 12.
* **Real bugs it found**: a lamp keeps the shape's material in pbrt and so reflects (13% dark in the worst scene); CPU alpha cutouts shaded what is seen through them as if it sat in the cutout's plane and cast no shadows; CPU curve strands were 17% too dark; a `dots` texture ignored its own mapping and pbrt's swapped colours.
* **Scene Builder**: 3D text, a Dots pattern and a Sky gallery.
* **Metal** (the Mac session): fog and coloured fog follow the CPU, sphere lights are sampled like pbrt's, a lamp reflects another lamp (it only reflected a sky: `emissive-sphere-occluder` read 7% dark, 1.000 of pbrt-v4 now), a dots texture counts its cells in its own mapping, an imagemap's `wrap` (clamp, black, repeat) is honoured, and a height map is read through a mip chain with the camera's footprint at every hit (the finite-difference step of every Metal height map had been 2.7 times too big at a 40 degree field of view). The Scene Builder's instanced scenes draw on a Mac like the plain ones, and a spawned ray starts on its own side of the geometric surface as in pbrt (a bump or normal map that tilts the shading normal far no longer traps the rays it sends below the surface: `bump-mapped-plane` 4% and `normal-mapped-plane` 3% bright against pbrt-v4, 1.00 now)), and a height map whose sides are not powers of two gets pbrt's power-of-two copy before its mip levels are built. `scripts/metal_pbrt_pack.py` renders pbrt's reference scenes on Metal and prints each mean as a ratio of pbrt's. Metal is better protected: its shaders are compiled offline by `scripts/check_metal_shaders.py` (a step of CI too, which has no GPU and used to notice nothing), three new shader unit tests (the mip lookup of a height map against the CPU's mipmap, pbrt's cone sampling of a sphere light, the side a spawned ray leaves from) and two new ctest entries (`metal_poc_cpu_gpu_sweep`, the pairs found wrong before; `metal_poc_pbrt_pack`, 144 scenes against pbrt-v4's own means with a stored baseline). A texture `"string filter"` that Metal cannot honour now says so. Coloured fog on Metal now matches the CPU in every channel (a wall's light sample through the fog sphere used the mean extinction while the path followed one colour: red read 3% low), a sphere with object motion blur streaks over its whole path and casts a smeared shadow (its box held only the start position and the shadow was cast from time 0), and a moving disk or cylinder, which Metal still draws still, says so. Measured BSDFs use the 18-wavelength quadrature on Metal as on the CPU (blue was 18% high there). The Metal shader file that was about to pass the 2,000-line limit (`metal_poc_sampling.metal`) is split into six by topic (environment lights, procedural textures, BSDF math, media math, area lights; code motion only, every test and the golden snapshot unchanged). The two longest Metal host functions are split too: the pbrt loader's sphere and punctual-light code (a sphere's material, its sphere light, the goniometric and projection images; the lights move to their own file) and `dsFillUniforms` (camera, medium, sky, filter); the six near-identical texture uploads share one helper. All 201 renderable scenes produce byte-identical pictures before and after. A bounced hit's bump-map footprint on Metal is pbrt's `Approximate_dp_dxy` (the camera's smallest pixel seen in the direction of the hit, not a camera ray through it; `bump-fine-floor` 0.989 of pbrt-v4), and the Metal-against-pbrt test is stricter: a scene missing from its baseline fails, a pinned error more than 3% off pbrt needs a written reason, and the drift gate is 1% instead of 4%. The Mac GUI self-test prepares the GPU renderer before it starts, so a new build passes on the first run (it used to fail the Rendered viewport once while Metal compiled). A measured BSDF renders 24 times faster on Metal (`measured-lights`: 31 s to 1.3 s at the pack's size; the 18-wavelength quadrature, as first ported, had made it slow): the spectral table is summed into RGB once on the host. An area light's image is read with clamp, as in pbrt (`textured-twosided-lights` blue 5% high to 0.7% low). The Mac parity test could not fail on a golden-snapshot drift (ctest looked only at the output, and the second worker process printed its own PASS): it fails now, and the snapshot is re-taken for the two scenes that moved on purpose (`colorspace-blackbody`, `textured-twosided-lights`). The Mac test suite no longer fails by chance or hangs: every test has a time limit (a parity run once sat for three hours on a GPU call that never returned, because ctest had no default), and the Metal tests take turns on the GPU (under `ctest -j4` macOS's watchdog killed renders that pass alone: four of seven full runs failed), at the cost of about 100 seconds (about six minutes in all). A failed render in these tests now prints the renderer's own output.

Known and recorded: both OptiX backends draw a cylinder-type curve as a flat-shaded 8-sided tube (+4% on the strand pixels, +0.6% on a whole picture); `maxcomponentvalue-firefly-clamp` reads +24% against pbrt because a per-sample clamp depends on how many light samples a vertex takes; coloured absorbing media read 3-4% bright on the CPU and recursive backend (RGB against pbrt's spectra); `environment-sibenik-cathedral` reads 9% dimmer on the GPU than on the CPU and `realistic-camera` is mirrored between them (both were there before); the Metal gaps this list once had (lamps that reflect, a dots texture's mapping) are closed; what Metal still does differently is listed under Known at the end of this section.

### Changed

* **The Live Preview says why it cannot render a scene.** It showed "scene may not be GPU-supported, or the wavefront backend is unavailable" for every failure. Now the renderer's own reason is shown: "Scene contains no geometry" for an empty scene, the mesh file it could not read (and where it looked) for a scene whose assets are missing, or that the renderer could not start; the generic text remains only for a failure the library cannot explain. A renderer that failed to start is no longer kept as the global one (every later frame used it unstarted).
* **The Windows Live Preview keeps the Resolution setting's aspect ratio** (the longer side is 400 pixels), as the Mac one already did. It was a fixed 400x300, which framed a square scene such as the Cornell boxes small, with black bars at the sides, and showed a 16:9 or portrait render with the wrong framing. Square, 16:9, odd sizes (400x267) and portrait (225x400) all render, with SVGF and temporal upscale on too.
* **Live Preview moves the camera by a fraction of the scene, not a fixed 20 units.** One press of W, A, S, D, Up or Down moved 20 units whatever the scene (tuned on the 555-unit Cornell box), so it flew out of a small room in one press. It is 2% of the scene's size now (11 units in the Cornell box, 0.13 in a 6-unit scene), and the closest zoom is a fraction of it too (it was a fixed 1 unit). The size is a new `# @rt-size` line in each scene's header (the largest side of its bounding box in the scene's own units): all 193 bundled scenes that can be measured have it (`scripts/stamp_scene_sizes.py`, `ray_tracer --print-scene-size`), the Scene Builder writes it for its own scenes, and a scene without one uses the camera's distance to its target. The keyboard sensitivity setting still scales the step.
* **Metal PNGs no longer get a vignette or a colour shift.** They were added to every Mac render (corners 18% darker, red and blue shifted apart) although the CPU and OptiX renderers add neither, so the same scene looked different on a Mac. They are opt-in now (`RT_METAL_POST_EFFECTS=vignette,aberration`); the light bilateral blur stays on by default until the Mac has a real denoiser.
* **AI denoising on a Mac.** The Metal renderer can now denoise with Intel's Open Image Denoise (`--denoise`, or the **AI denoiser** box in Render Options): a render with a handful of samples per pixel comes out clean. The library (~50 MB) is not inside the app: ticking the box offers to download it once from OIDN's own release page (checksum-verified). See [docs/DENOISING.md](docs/DENOISING.md).
* `--maxcomponentvalue` (the explicit firefly clamp) now works on Metal too.
* **Render passes (AOVs).** `--aovs` (or **Also write render passes** in Render Options) writes albedo, normal, depth, uv and a coverage alpha next to the image: merged into the same file for an `.exr` output (a multilayer EXR with the render's R G B), else into `<name>.aovs.exr`. Works with every renderer. See [docs/RENDER_PASSES.md](docs/RENDER_PASSES.md).

* **One table of what each renderer reads** (`src/shared/backend_capabilities.h`): the command line's "has no effect" warnings and the greyed-out Render Options controls both come from it. This fixed `--adaptive` being treated as ignored on Metal, which does read it. [docs/BACKEND_SUPPORT.md](docs/BACKEND_SUPPORT.md) is the same facts for people: CPU, both OptiX renderers and Metal in one table.
* **Live Preview controls follow the renderer's real features**, not the platform: the library says which optional features it has (`realtime_backend_features()`), and its C interface is declared once (`src/shared/realtime_api.h`) instead of copied into the GUI and each library.
* **The Scene Builder log is more complete**: a deleted object is named correctly, the last edit is written before a render and on quit, restoring the unsaved scene and a failing backup write are logged, and so are selections, the 3D tool keys, scene problems and meshes the 3D view cannot read. [docs/LOGGING.md](docs/LOGGING.md).

* **The OptiX renderers now draw cones and paraboloids** (as a triangle approximation, the same step the Metal renderer uses; `src/shared/gpu_tessellate.h`) instead of dropping them with a warning. The cone and paraboloid gallery (F14) matches the CPU render (mean 0.431 vs 0.429). The scale/centre/offset rule the Mac loader places a scene in is shared too (`gpu_scene_frame.h`), the first step of [docs/GPU_SCENE_COMMON.md](docs/GPU_SCENE_COMMON.md).

* **Every scene has a stable name** (`cornell-box`, a scene file's name) next to its short id (`A1`, `K37`). Ids still work everywhere, but they move when a category is added or a file is added to `pbrt_scenes/`; names do not. The GUI saves names (recent renders, thumbnails, notes), and the command line accepts either.

The 58 bundled test fixtures are a **Test Scenes** category; Custom Scenes holds your own and downloaded scenes. ### Rendering correctness

* **A cloud or grid medium fills its interface sphere on the CPU** (it filled the whole box its density is declared in, so a cloud whose cube circumscribed the sphere rendered as a cube while OptiX and Metal drew the sphere). Cloud, rgbgrid and uniformgrid media are cut to the sphere (`medium_clip.h`), for free flights and shadow rays alike.
* **OptiX consistency sweep (first run on Windows, recursive and wavefront backends, 472 pairs, 0 off)**: subsurface read 5-12% dark on OptiX (the exit BSDF got the probe-facing normal instead of the shape's outward one, the exit sampled no point/spot/distant lights, and shadow rays counted a subsurface shape as an occluder where the CPU sees through it); a grid or cloud under a sphere read 6% dark (shadow tracking ran through the whole grid box, not the interface sphere's chord); an instanced fog ball was not built at all, its medium chord ignored the instance transform, and a scene made only of instances stopped with "Scene contains no geometry". See [docs/CONSISTENCY_SWEEP.md](docs/CONSISTENCY_SWEEP.md).
* **The Windows GUI no longer crashes on exit after a Live Preview** (crash code 0xC0000409 as the program closed, which Windows may report as "stopped working"). The OptiX renderer object was destroyed during static destruction, after the CUDA runtime had already been unloaded; it is now left for the operating system to release, which is all its destructor did at that point.
* **The Windows GUI no longer aborts when a renderer library prints an error.** The GUI sends the process's stderr into its log file, and on Windows that step always failed (the log is already open) after it had closed stderr, so the first line any renderer library wrote there ended the program at once (crash code 0xC0000409, no message). A Live Preview of an empty scene was one way to reach it: the OptiX renderer's "Scene contains no geometry" line. The redirect now opens a second handle and duplicates it onto stderr (it cannot close stderr any more), the preview of a scene the GPU cannot render reports "Render failed" and retries twice a second instead of spinning (and writing that line to the log hundreds of times a second), and the `ui` GUI self-test writes a line to stderr so a regression crashes it.
* **Subsurface scattering on a Mac.** pbrt's `subsurface` material (marble, skin, wax as a real BSSRDF) rendered as a grey diffuse on Metal. It is now the real thing: a smooth dielectric boundary, the tabulated diffusion profile built by the same code as the CPU's, a probe walk that finds the point where the light leaves, and the normalized-Fresnel exit, ported from OptiX. Shadow rays pass through a subsurface surface, as on the CPU and OptiX. Where an area light lights the object it matches the CPU within 1-2% (new `subsurface-ball.pbrt`, and B16 / layered-materials); under only a constant sky it comes out 15-20% darker inside the ball. New Mac tests: `metal_poc_subsurface`, and a shader test of the profile math against the CPU's.
* **Alpha cutouts on a Mac.** A pbrt shape's `"texture alpha"` mask (leaves, fences, foliage in the big environment scenes) was ignored by Metal, so cut-out geometry rendered as solid quads. Metal now tests the mask like the CPU and OptiX do (pbrt's stochastic alpha test); only scenes that have a mask pay for it. New scene `alpha-cutout.pbrt` and a Mac test.
* **Mix materials blend per hit on a Mac.** A pbrt `mix` of two materials used to render as just one of them on Metal (B15's sphere was pure copper instead of red-and-copper). The shader now picks one of the two at every hit, the second with probability `amount`, like the CPU and OptiX; B15 matches the CPU to 1%.
* **Dispersion on a Mac.** A pbrt dielectric with `"float abbenumber"` (the glass prisms B23 and B24) now selects Metal's dispersive glass: B23 shows the colour fan the CPU's `--spectral` render has, using three wavelengths like the OptiX recursive backend. The shader had it all along; the loader never chose it.
* **Clouds now render on a Mac.** A pbrt scene's `cloud` medium (E2, E5) showed no cloud on Metal at all: the loader had no case for it. It does now, and the picture matches the CPU's (within 0.5% over the whole cloud) and its density has the CPU's wispiness warp. Three shader faults it exposed are fixed in the same change: rays that had just left the cloud's box re-entered the shader without moving (a dark fringe), the sky-light rays used the box's maximum density instead of the real one (a black cloud), and the sky light was counted twice after a scatter (too bright).
* **Path tracers add the last continuation ray.** The CPU and both OptiX backends stopped one segment early, so the emission or sky seen by the last
  bounce was lost: a diffuse sphere (albedo 0.5) under a white sky read 0.09 at depth 1 instead of 0.5, and a Cornell box was 1% / 3% / 10% dark at depth 8 / 4 / 2.
* **Measured BRDFs** now use pbrt's path weight `f * cos / pdf` (a white-furnace sphere read 7.8 / 8.1 / 3.5 instead of 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4) and take direct-light samples with MIS.
* **Diffuse transmission** was wrong in three ways (lobe probability counted twice on the CPU, missing on the GPU, shadow rays passing through the shell) and the GPU never lit it from a
  point, spot or distant light. It matches the closed form of a closed shell (0.65 in the limit) on every backend, and a plate under a point light reads its closed form.
* **Coloured participating media** are sampled per colour channel on the CPU and both OptiX backends (they used one luminance extinction: a blue-heavy absorber read grey 0.47 instead of 0.82 / 0.45 / 0.17).
* **GPU sphere lights far from the shading point** no longer lose light to float32 cancellation (a light 480 units away read 65% / 73% of its closed form; now within 0.1%).
* **Cone and paraboloid lights**: the path tracer's light-sampling density missed the second crossing of a ray (3.4x too bright next to a cone light), and the paraboloid's surface area was wrong.
* **Image sky orientation** was inconsistent between the CPU's lookup and its own importance-sampling table, and between backends; all now put image row 0 straight up.

### Alternative integrators

* **BDPT and MLT**: fixed a never-set reverse density (8-25% dark in closed rooms), sphere and two-sided emitter densities, distant-light and infinite-light vertices, glossy and refracting BSDF handling, and
  pixel-filter use; scattering fog read 0.59x too dark and now matches. Disk, cylinder, cone and paraboloid lights now work. MLT was confirmed unbiased.
* **Coloured fog** under BDPT, MLT, SPPM and the debug integrators is rendered as three per-channel passes (three times the render time; they say so).
* **SPPM**: photon passes use the adjoint BSDF, the CPU gather radius scales with the scene (a fixed 10 units read 0.30 instead of 0.65 in an 8-unit room), and it warns about media (it has no volume model, like pbrt-v4's).
* **GPU SPPM** samples textures, handles a uniform sky, varies its samples per iteration, supports orthographic and equirectangular cameras and depth of field, and rebuilds its shader table when the scene
  changes (it read 0.33-0.47x of the path tracer on textured or sky-lit scenes and could crash the CUDA context).
* **`--simplepath`, `--randomwalk`, `--lightpath`** follow the same conventions as the main integrators; a visible sphere or cylinder light in `--lightpath` read 0.42x / 0.52x and now matches.

un_optix_sweeps.ps1` builds the renderer and runs the consistency sweep on both OptiX backends (recursive and wavefront) in one command, ending with a PASS or FAIL line each.

* A `LICENSE` (MIT), [third-party notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md), a README gallery, a CPU-only quick start, [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md), issue and pull request templates, and a code of conduct.

### Known gaps

* GPU SPPM's gather radius is a fixed 5 units; SPPM emits photons from area lights only (sky-lit scenes read 2-3% low); one SPPM scene (A7, Perlin spheres) reads about 10% low on CPU and GPU alike.
* `--lightpath` has not been checked in participating media. BDPT, MLT and SPPM do not support camera media; BDPT/MLT/SPPM do not support portal lights.
* GPU BDPT/MLT do not exist. Linux and a native arm64 macOS GUI are not supported yet.
* A NanoVDB `temperaturename` blackbody glow renders on the CPU only (the GPU renders the density and warns).
* A two-level nested texture (a checkerboard inside a checkerboard that holds an image) is flattened to one average colour on the GPU, with a warning (scene J6).
